Dear Editors of Array,

Please consider “Failure Boundaries of Censored Passive Feedback in Authenticated Wireless Sensor Routing.” The central contribution is a controlled characterization of conditional feedback performance and an exposed adaptation limitation after relay behavior changes. The paper retains matched controls, negative policy comparisons and explicit transport and execution boundaries. It does not estimate a universal failure threshold or introduce a new learning algorithm.

The prespecified 750-run study uses 30 new fixtures. Under blackholes, delivery improves by 4.67 percentage points relative to active proofs (paired 95% interval 2.36–6.97; Holm-adjusted p=0.0161). Matched controls do not establish a unique censoring or attention advantage. A separate 180-run authenticated transfer study likewise establishes no attention superiority. A separately frozen 240-run factorial extension identifies a proof-traffic delivery penalty under always-on listening, with severe low-duty limitations.

A separate 600-run Cooja study now executes the frozen actor and weighted feedback ledger under matched proof-generation controls. It exposes a post-warm-up blackhole failure: with scheduled processing, active proofs deliver 79.11% versus 32.94% for censored feedback. This counterexample limits the original replacement result rather than supporting universal superiority.

A four-node emulated MCU pilot also integrates inference, hop authentication and signed receipts. Q22 inference reduces original signed-receipt deadline misses from 134/200 to 68/200. A separate private source–sink MAC receipt meets the tested source deadlines while retaining deferred Ed25519 proofs; it changes the endpoint and adds traffic. We make no physical RF, measured energy, protected hardware key-storage or deployment-readiness claim.

Code, checkpoints, raw traces, negative results, firmware and audits are available at https://github.com/DrShivamBhardwaj/JICCE_LA_CPPO_Reproducibility.

The manuscript was substantially redesigned following rejection of JICCE-26-0057. The present controlled feedback study replaces the earlier optimization/intrusion-detection framing and does not claim validated live intrusion detection.
