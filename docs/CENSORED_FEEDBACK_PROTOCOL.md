# Frozen receipt assisted passive feedback experiment

The previous telemetry study, seeds 76001–76030, is development evidence for this redesign and is not reused for inference. Its adverse feedback-control results remain in the repository. The present design is frozen before evaluating seeds 77001–77030. Pilot 76901 is excluded.

The proposed change is a receipt-assisted, censored evidence ledger. It emits no separate forwarding-proof frames. An observer records a selected relay and packet identifier, waits for its own MAC acknowledgment, and accepts actual overheard forwarding as half-strength positive evidence. An existing reverse delivery receipt upgrades that same observation to full-strength evidence. Duplicate retransmissions or receipts cannot exceed one link-reliability-weighted unit per observer/relay/packet. A failed first link or missing observation is censored rather than counted as an attack. A late receipt may resolve a previously censored record. This is an evidence preference score, not an estimated attack probability or calibrated IDS.

Five arms share frozen checkpoint pairing, traffic, beacons, radio settings, reverse receipts, current model and local feature builder: censored passive attention, naive passive attention with timeout penalties, the earlier active-proof attention, a frozen-prior attention control with no evidence updates, and capacity-matched MLP with the censored ledger. The frozen-prior control retains the same initial risk coordinates rather than setting risk to zero, isolating evidence updates from the prior-feature convention. It is not the previous risk-disabled arm. Passive observations use actual promiscuous MAC receive callbacks, not simulator relay truth. No extra listening state is enabled because every arm already keeps its radio listening; CPU overhead and sleep-mode portability remain unvalidated.

The naive control shares passive/receipt evidence and per-packet deduplication but treats missing acknowledged forwarding as negative evidence. The active-proof arm preserves the earlier proof/timeout update convention. These contrasts separate censoring, proof traffic and architecture as far as the specified controls allow; active versus passive also changes the evidence source, so it is not a pure airtime intervention.

The protocol JSON fixes all five traffic/attack/link settings, 60 source packets, four-second radio horizon, 500 ms beacon period, 750 ms cache lifetime, 150 ms watch interval and current assumptions. Thirty fixtures × five arms × five settings produce 750 runs and 45,000 generated packets. Existing attention and capacity-MLP checkpoints trained on 41001–41030 remain frozen. No outcome-based seed, checkpoint or parameter selection is permitted.

All 60 paired two-sided tests (five settings × four comparators × three endpoints) form one Holm family. Paired 95% effect intervals are unadjusted. The decision gate requires a positive Holm-significant blackhole delivery gain over active proofs plus all three blackhole non-inferiority checks against frozen-prior attention: delivery lower one-sided 95% bound above −1 pp, critical-violation upper bound below +1 pp, and geometric radio-energy-per-delivered-byte upper ratio below 1.02. All requirements must pass. These are incremental mechanism criteria, not proof of the original absolute service budgets. A failed gate is retained.

Passive watchdog observation is established prior art. Marti, Giuli, Lai and Baker, “Mitigating routing misbehavior in mobile ad hoc networks,” MobiCom 2000, DOI 10.1145/345910.345955, describe overhearing and its collision, receiver-loss and collusion ambiguities. The present experiment does not claim invention of watchdog monitoring. Its research question concerns evidence censoring and charged control costs within this frozen routing pipeline. Primary paper: https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~ebelding/courses/290I/w03/papers/Marti_Mitigating.pdf . The ns-3 API is documented at https://www.nsnam.org/doxygen/d6/d13/classns3_1_1lrwpan_1_1_lr_wpan_mac.html .

The threat model remains non-forging blackhole relays with trusted beacon and receipt producers. Authentication, malicious beacons, receiver-targeted power attacks, collusion, selective replay, live IDS and physical hardware validation are outside the evaluated claim. Positive passive evidence proves a forwarding attempt, not downstream reception; a delivery receipt supplies stronger, path-level evidence. No novelty or superiority conclusion is available until the frozen evaluation is completed.
